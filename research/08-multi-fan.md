# Multiple Ceiling Fans in One Large Room or Hall — Evidence Dossier (AtomQuest 2026)

## 1. Condition definition

- **Geometry:** Two or more ceiling fans sharing one open volume — a large living-dining room, a hall, a classroom, an office floor, a banquet/prayer hall, or a warehouse. The defining parameters are: room floor area, fan sweep diameter (D), number of fans (n), centre-to-centre spacing (S) and spacing-to-diameter ratio (S/D), and whether fans run at matched or different speeds/directions.
- **Practical thresholds from industry guidance:**
  - CBE Berkeley's Advanced Ceiling Fan Design Tool / fan guidebook recommends subdividing a large room into equal, roughly square **"fan cells"** (aspect ratio ≤ 1.5:1 per cell) and centring one fan per cell. Fan diameter should be **0.2–0.4 × the characteristic room width**, and fan centre-to-centre spacing should be **≥ 2.5 × D**. Minimum blade-to-ceiling clearance is **20 cm or 0.2×D** (whichever is larger); minimum blade-to-wall clearance **45 cm**; minimum mounting height **2.1 m** above floor. [CBE Fans Guidebook – Ceiling fan installation](https://cbe-berkeley.gitbook.io/fans-guidebook/full-guidebook/ceiling-fan-installation-and-integration-with-hvac-system), [CBE Design goals and fan selection](https://cbe-berkeley.gitbook.io/fans-guidebook/full-guidebook/design-goals-and-fan-selection), [CBE Advanced Ceiling Fan Design Tool](https://cbe.berkeley.edu/research/advanced-ceiling-fan-design-tool)
  - HVLS (high-volume low-speed) industrial guidance (Big Ass Fans, Hunter Industrial) recommends **3–5× D** centre spacing for large industrial fans, and keeping the fan centreline **≥ 1.5× D** off walls; example: 24-ft (7.3 m) fans spaced 60–120 ft apart. [Hunter Industrial — fan layout](https://industrialfans.hunterfan.com/blogs/hunter-industrial-blog/strategic-placement-of-hvls-fans-for-optimal-effectiveness), [Big Ass Fans Design Guide](https://learn.bigassfans.com/design-guide)
  - Residential/Indian retail guidance (Atomberg) is cruder: for halls **> 225 sq ft (~21 m²)**, install two fans with **≥ 4 ft (1.2 m) blade-tip-to-blade-tip** clearance; a single 1400 mm fan centred in a long rectangular room leaves corners stagnant, so two 1200 mm fans are recommended instead. [Atomberg — Ceiling Fan Size Guide](https://atomberg.com/blogs/blog/ceiling-fan-size-chart)
- **Prevalence in India [UNVERIFIED — no national survey found]:** Multi-fan rooms are extremely common — virtually every Indian living-dining hall, classroom, lecture hall, office floor-plate, marriage/banquet hall, temple hall and railway-platform waiting area uses 2 or more ceiling fans. A verified field study used **11 naturally ventilated Indian university lecture halls with 828 student samples**, each with multiple fans, confirming the ubiquity of multi-fan halls in Indian institutional buildings. [Influence of non-uniform distribution of fan-induced air on thermal comfort in university classrooms in warm-humid India — ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132323004006). A specific example: IIT Bombay classrooms use **four ceiling fans plus two exhaust fans** per room [UNVERIFIED exact source, reported in search aggregation of the above study]. No BEE/census-level statistic on the fraction of Indian rooms with >1 fan was found; treat "very common" as directional, not quantified.

## 2. Aerodynamic physics

- **Single-fan baseline problem:** A lone fan produces high air speed directly beneath it and at floor/desk level within its footprint, but speed falls off sharply outside that zone — poor spatial uniformity over a large or elongated room. This motivates using multiple fans rather than one oversized fan. [Schiavon — Interaction of ceiling fans, chamber study](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/interaction-ceiling-fans-results-from-chamber-study-stefano-schiavon), [Detailed experimental investigation of air speed field induced by ceiling fans — ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360132318303809)
- **Interaction mechanisms when fans are close:**
  1. **Merging downwash cones / recirculation-cell competition.** Each fan drives a toroidal recirculation loop (downwash core → floor spread → wall-ward return → ceiling-ward return flow). When two cells sit close enough, their return flows meet in the inter-fan region, either reinforcing (merging into a broader, more uniform floor-level flow) or interfering (shear-layer turbulence, local velocity deficits) depending on relative rotation direction, phase and speed.
  2. **Turbulent shear zone between fans.** The return-flow boundary between two adjacent downwash jets is a free shear layer; mismatched fan speeds amplify turbulence intensity there, which can increase local gustiness and noise without raising mean velocity.
  3. **Same vs opposite rotation:** No standard residential wiring supports true phase/direction synchronisation; in practice fans run independently and rotation sense has negligible net effect on far-field pattern because the dominant structure is the axisymmetric downwash/return pair, not a single coherent swirl that could constructively/destructively interfere at room scale. [UNVERIFIED — no study found that isolates same- vs counter-rotation effect at room scale; this is inferred from the documented physics of single-fan recirculation and the one documented multi-fan study, not from a direct same/opposite-rotation experiment.]
  4. **Non-linear, non-superposable combined field.** The first dedicated multi-fan chamber study (NUS/Schiavon et al., climatic chamber, 5,760 measurement points single-fan / 20,160 points double-fan) found that **the combined air-speed profile of two interacting fans cannot be extrapolated (is not a simple sum or average) from the single-fan profile** — both the *difference in fan speed levels* and the *distance between fans* affect the interaction in complex, non-linear ways. When fans are close enough and run at similar speed, the previously documented "high speed under fan, low speed outside" non-uniformity problem is **significantly reduced**. [Schiavon et al. — Interaction of ceiling fans: chamber study](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/interaction-ceiling-fans-results-from-chamber-study-stefano-schiavon); underlying full paper: [Detailed experimental investigation of air speed field induced by ceiling fans — eScholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2mk3n264/qt2mk3n264_noSplash_de02101012eb8ed43abfd4ebb4e2d627.pdf)
- **Governing non-dimensional parameters (standard fan aerodynamics, applied to the multi-fan case):**
  - **Spacing ratio S/D** (centre-to-centre spacing ÷ fan diameter) — the primary design variable; CBE recommends S/D ≥ 2.5 for residential/commercial fans, HVLS guidance uses 3–5 for large industrial fans.
  - **Diameter-to-room-width ratio D/W** — CBE recommends 0.2–0.4.
  - **Aspect ratio of each "fan cell"** — recommended ≤ 1.5:1; above this, a single fan under-serves the cell and a second fan is needed.
  - Standard single-fan parameters (Reynolds number at blade, actuator-disk thrust, jet spread rate, Richardson/Archimedes number for buoyancy competition) still apply locally to each fan but are modulated by the neighbouring fan's induced flow field — i.e., each fan effectively sees a non-uniform, time-varying inflow/ambient condition set by its neighbour, rather than quiescent room air.
- **Quantified effects:** The Schiavon/Berkeley chamber study is the only source found with directly quantified multi-fan interaction data (air-speed maps at thousands of points for 1-fan vs 2-fan cases); exact % uniformity improvement numbers were not extracted from the search snippets — **[UNVERIFIED numeric value; the full paper (eScholarship PDF above) should be read in full for the specific percentages]**. No source quantifying noise dB(A) change, wobble, or power draw specifically from fan-to-fan interaction was found — **[UNVERIFIED / gap]**.

## 3. Role of speed / RPM / frequency

- Matching fan speeds across all units in a cell grid is the central recommendation implicit in the CBE fan-cell method ("when all fans operate at the same speed, individual fan cells should target aspect ratio ≤ 1.5:1") — mismatched speeds between neighbours change the interaction pattern unpredictably per the Schiavon finding that "both the difference of fan speed levels and the distance between fans" drive the combined profile. [CBE Fans Guidebook](https://cbe-berkeley.gitbook.io/fans-guidebook/full-guidebook/design-goals-and-fan-selection); [Schiavon chamber study](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/interaction-ceiling-fans-results-from-chamber-study-stefano-schiavon)
- **Synchronised control is a known gap in residential practice:** standard Indian/residential wall-mounted electronic regulators are not designed to drive multiple fans evenly — when two fans share one switch/regulator, voltage drop along the wiring run causes the fan electrically closer to the regulator to spin faster than the farther one, and low/medium speed steps may not modulate correctly for both. [DIYChatroom — Dual ceiling fan speed control](https://www.diychatroom.com/threads/dual-ceiling-fan-in-parallel-speed-control.191233/), [Mike Holt forums — 4 fans on 1 speed control](https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/4-ceiling-fans-on-1-fan-speed-control.129788/)
- **Smart/IoT synchronisation exists commercially:** Atomberg's BLDC fans with IoT connectivity allow simultaneous app/voice control of multiple fans so they can at least be commanded to the same speed setting (though true closed-loop RPM/phase sync across units is a separate, harder problem). [Atomberg size guide — multi-fan smart control](https://atomberg.com/blogs/blog/ceiling-fan-size-chart)
- **No rotation-direction or reverse-mode literature found specific to multi-fan rooms** — updraft/reverse mode discussions in the general literature address single-fan winter circulation, not multi-fan interaction. [UNVERIFIED / gap].
- **Patented industrial precedent:** Large air-handling "fan array" systems (data-centre/AHU scale, not ceiling fans) use an array controller that brings individual fan units online/offline and tunes RPM to hold an efficient combined operating point — US10317105B2, US20110014061A1, US8849463B2 (Google Patents). These establish that synchronised/array speed control is an active, patented engineering domain, though applied to axial cooling-fan arrays rather than room ceiling fans. [US10317105B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10317105B2/en), [US20110014061A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20110014061A1/en), [US8849463B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8849463B2/ko)
- A residential ceiling-fan-specific wireless multi-fan control patent exists: **WO2015171729A1 "Ceiling fan control system"** — a system/method to control and manipulate functional features of a plurality of ceiling fans in a defined location via a wireless device. [WO2015171729A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2015171729A1)

## 4. Blade and fan geometry levers

Ranked by expected impact for the multi-fan condition specifically (not for single-fan performance, which is covered in other condition briefs):

1. **Fan diameter relative to cell size (D/W ratio, 0.2–0.4 target)** — directly sets how many fans are needed and the resulting S/D; wrong sizing (one oversized fan instead of two correctly sized ones) is the single biggest layout error reported in Indian retail guidance. [Atomberg size guide](https://atomberg.com/blogs/blog/ceiling-fan-size-chart)
2. **Matched sweep size and matched aerodynamic output (air-delivery curve) across all units in the room** — mismatched models create asymmetric cell sizes and uneven floor coverage; this is an installation/selection lever rather than a single-fan blade-design lever, but it dominates multi-fan outcomes.
3. **Downwash footprint shape (circular vs non-circular jet).** All current residential fans produce a circular/elliptical footprint set by blade sweep; no commercial product was found with an intentionally "squared-off" or directional footprint optimised to tile a room with minimal gaps or overlap — a possible novel lever (see Section 9).
4. **Blade tip design / tip-vortex strength** — a weaker, faster-decaying tip vortex would reduce the shear-layer turbulence generated where two fans' return flows meet, potentially cutting noise in the inter-fan zone, but no source quantifies this for the multi-fan case **[UNVERIFIED, inferred from general tip-vortex aerodynamics, not multi-fan-specific evidence]**.
5. **Hub/motor housing size** — secondary; affects only the small near-hub dead zone within each fan's own footprint, not inter-fan interaction.

## 9. Unsolved gaps and opportunities

- **Gap 1 — No consumer-facing, India-specific multi-fan layout tool.** CBE's calculator and HVLS design guides exist but target US commercial buildings; Indian brands give only rule-of-thumb clearances (e.g., "4 ft blade-tip gap for >225 sq ft"). **Opportunity:** an app-based layout calculator (room dimensions → fan count, size, spacing) built on the CBE fan-cell method (D/W 0.2–0.4, S/D ≥2.5, cell aspect ≤1.5:1), localised for common Indian room sizes/sweeps (900–1400 mm). Low cost, no new hardware, moderate novelty (software/service, limited patentability but strong product differentiation).
- **Gap 2 — No true multi-fan speed/phase synchronisation in the Indian residential market.** Existing "smart" control (Atomberg IoT) issues the same command to multiple fans but doesn't address the voltage-drop-induced speed mismatch on shared legacy regulators, nor coordinate fans to actively balance a room's airflow (e.g., auto-reducing the speed of a fan whose cell is already well-served by a neighbour's spillover). **Opportunity:** BLDC controllers with mesh/app communication that read a shared target air-speed-uniformity goal and individually trim each fan's RPM — a natural extension of existing BLDC electronics, moderate cost impact (firmware + comms, no new mechanical parts), moderate novelty, some overlap risk with the AHU fan-array patents above (design around by making the optimisation target "occupant comfort/airspeed uniformity" rather than "electrical efficiency").
- **Gap 3 — No data-driven inter-fan spacing/noise trade-off for Indian room sizes and ceiling heights.** The only measured multi-fan interaction dataset (Schiavon et al.) is a lab chamber study; Indian halls have lower ceilings, smaller rooms, and different fan models (1200 mm BLDC vs US products) than tested. **Opportunity:** Atomberg-sponsored CFD + chamber study replicating the Schiavon methodology at Indian room scale (e.g., 3 m × 8 m hall, 2× 1200 mm fans, 2.7 m ceiling) to produce a validated, India-specific spacing/uniformity/noise dataset — directly fills the Section 7 evidence gap and differentiates on "tested, not assumed" claims. Moderate cost (lab time), high novelty as a published dataset, low patent risk (it's data/methodology, not a product claim per se).
- **Gap 4 — No fan footprint shape optimised for tiling a room.** All current blades produce a circular/elliptical downwash; tiling a rectangular hall with circular footprints inherently leaves corner/edge gaps (acknowledged even in Atomberg's own marketing copy about "a single fan leaves corners stagnant"). **Opportunity [speculative, higher risk]:** asymmetric blade length/pitch per blade position, or an intentionally elliptical multi-blade design, to better tile rectangular cells with less overlap — higher engineering risk (balance, vibration), and uncertain benefit magnitude; recommend only after Gap 3's dataset exists to quantify the current gap size.
- **Gap 5 — No guidance on mixed-orientation / non-grid multi-fan rooms** (e.g., irregular open-plan Indian living-dining-kitchen layouts, L-shaped halls with 2–3 fans not in a clean grid). The CBE method assumes a rectangular grid of square cells; real Indian homes rarely are that regular. **Opportunity:** extend the fan-cell method with simple correction factors for L-shaped/open-plan rooms — overlaps with Condition Brief 11 (open-plan/L-shaped spaces); worth cross-referencing in the eventual cross-condition synthesis (S1).

## 10. Measurement and validation plan

- **Instrumentation:** a grid of **vane or hot-wire/hot-sphere anemometers** (CBE/Schiavon-style studies use dense grids — their chamber work used 5,760–20,160 points via a traversing probe, which is beyond a hackathon budget; a practical replication can use **a handheld anemometer on a tripod moved through a defined grid**, e.g., 0.5 m spacing, at three heights: 0.1 m ankle, 1.1 m seated head, 1.7 m standing head, per ASHRAE 55 occupant-level definitions). [ASHRAE 55 occupant-level definitions — Mississippi Valley ASHRAE slide deck](https://www.mississippivalleyashrae.org/docs/ASHRAE-55.pdf)
- **Smoke/fog visualisation** at the boundary between two fans' footprints to directly observe the shear-layer/merging behaviour described in Section 2, replicating the Schiavon validation method qualitatively.
- **Sound meter** readings at the same grid points to check whether the inter-fan shear zone is audibly noisier than under either fan alone (a gap identified in Section 2 with no existing quantified data).
- **Power meter** per fan to check whether proximity/interaction changes motor loading (BLDC controllers may show altered current draw if inflow is disturbed by a neighbour's downwash reaching up to the blade's inlet side) — speculative mechanism, worth testing cheaply.
- **Test matrix:** vary S/D (e.g., 1.5, 2.5, 3.5, 4.5×D) and relative fan speed (matched vs mismatched by one step) in a real Indian-scale room (e.g., 3×8 m hall, 2.7 m ceiling, two 1200 mm fans) — directly fills the Gap 3 evidence need.
- **CFD comparison:** build the test room geometry in CFD using the validated single-fan approach (sliding mesh or overset mesh, SST k–ω turbulence model per the 2026 CFD validation paper) with two independently rotating fan zones; validate against the measured grid data above before trusting CFD-only spacing optimisation.

## 11. Sensors and smart control

- **Can a fan detect a multi-fan condition?** No existing product was found that senses a *neighbouring fan's* airflow and adapts; existing smart control (Atomberg IoT) is command-based (user or app sets all fans to the same speed), not sensor-adaptive.
- **Plausible sensing approaches [UNVERIFIED feasibility, no implementation found]:**
  - Current-draw/torque sensing on a BLDC motor could in principle detect when inflow is disturbed (e.g., by a neighbouring fan's return flow reaching the blade's upper surface), analogous to how BLDC controllers already compensate for voltage fluctuation (Condition Brief 26) — speculative extension, not demonstrated for multi-fan detection specifically.
  - App-level coordination (already commercial, Atomberg) is the only verified "smart multi-fan" feature in the Indian market; it is command-synchronisation, not sensor-based adaptive balancing.
  - IMU-based wobble detection (documented for single-fan vibration diagnostics in Condition Brief 28) has no demonstrated extension to detecting inter-fan aerodynamic interaction.
- This remains a largely **unaddressed area** — the clearest sensor-driven opportunity identified in Section 9 (Gap 2: airspeed-uniformity-aware RPM trimming) would require a new type of sensing (room air-speed or occupant comfort feedback, e.g., via a cheap ultrasonic or thermal array, or synced phone-app crowd feedback) that no current product implements.

## 12. Summary

**Executive summary:** Multiple ceiling fans sharing one hall or large room is an extremely common Indian scenario, but the engineering evidence base for it is thin and split between two worlds: a rigorous but lab-scale, two-fan-only academic study (Schiavon et al., Berkeley/Singapore chamber) that proved fan interaction is real, non-linear, and *beneficial for uniformity when fans are close and speed-matched*; and an informal, rule-of-thumb layout practice in the Indian residential market (clearance in feet, no published test data) alongside a more rigorous but foreign-market HVLS industrial design-guide tradition (CBE Berkeley's fan-cell method, Big Ass Fans' spacing guide) that has not been adapted to Indian ceiling-fan sizes or room scales. No Indian brand publishes a layout calculator, no brand demonstrates sensor-adaptive multi-fan balancing beyond simultaneous app commands, and only one patent family (WO2015171729A1) squarely covers multi-ceiling-fan control — leaving real white space for both a data-generation project (replicate the Schiavon method at Indian hall scale) and a product feature (airspeed-uniformity-aware synchronised BLDC control).

**Top 5 insights:**
1. Fan interaction is physically non-trivial: a two-fan combined air-speed field is *not* a simple sum of two single-fan fields — it depends on fan-speed mismatch and spacing in complex ways (Schiavon et al., Building and Environment 2018).
2. The best-documented sizing rule (CBE Berkeley): fan diameter 0.2–0.4× room width, spacing ≥2.5×D, fan-cell aspect ratio ≤1.5:1 — but this is a US commercial-building tool, unadapted to Indian 900–1400 mm residential fans and typical 2.6–3.2 m ceilings.
3. Indian residential practice is currently rule-of-thumb only (e.g., "≥4 ft blade-tip clearance beyond 225 sq ft"), with no published Indian test data behind it.
4. Synchronised multi-fan speed control is a real, documented pain point (shared-regulator voltage drop causes uneven speeds) that smart/BLDC/IoT control can solve, and Atomberg already offers app-level simultaneous control — but no brand does sensor-adaptive balancing.
5. The patent landscape for *ceiling-fan-specific* multi-fan control is thin (essentially one directly relevant family, WO2015171729A1); most "fan array" patents found are for AHU/electronics cooling fans, suggesting room for a differentiated, comfort-metric-driven multi-fan control patent.

**Open questions / what could not be found:**
- Exact numeric uniformity-improvement and noise/power figures from the Schiavon multi-fan study (only qualitative findings were retrieved from search snippets; the full paper should be read).
- Any quantified same-direction vs opposite-direction rotation effect at room scale.
- Brand-by-brand verified (non-marketing) specs for Crompton, Havells, Orient, Usha, Bajaj, Polycab, Luminous, Superfan, Gorilla, Panasonic multi-fan-specific features — this pass only substantiated Atomberg's app-control claim.
- A complete, India-specific (InPASS) patent search was not performed in this pass — only Google Patents/Justia global results were reviewed, and most were AHU/electronics cooling-fan patents, not ceiling fans.
- No source quantifying Indian-prevalence of multi-fan rooms (% of homes/halls) was found; "very common" is asserted from institutional/retail context only, not a cited statistic.
