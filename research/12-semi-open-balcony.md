# AtomQuest 2026 — Research Dossier: Semi-Open Spaces (Balconies, Verandas, Sit-Outs, Covered Terraces)

*Prepared for Atomberg "AtomQuest 2026" ceiling-fan aerodynamics hackathon track. Condition: fan operating in a semi-outdoor space with one or more open sides, exposed to ambient wind, dust, rain spray and high humidity.*

## 1. Condition definition

**Geometry/parameters.** A semi-open space is a roofed but partially-walled volume: typically one wall missing entirely (balcony, open sit-out) or two-to-three sides open with a parapet (veranda, covered terrace). Typical Indian dimensions: balconies 1.2–2.5 m deep × 2–4 m wide; verandas/sit-outs 2–4 m deep × 3–8 m long; covered terraces can be much larger and fully open on all sides except the roof slab. Ceiling height is usually the same 2.6–3.2 m as adjoining indoor rooms. Fan sweep is typically the same 900–1200 mm range, sometimes a smaller 600–900 mm "wall fan" format bolted to a wall bracket rather than a ceiling.

**How common in India.** Verandas/sit-outs and balconies with a ceiling fan are extremely common in independent houses and ground-floor units across India (traditional "sit-out" porches in Kerala/Tamil Nadu, "baithak" verandas in North India, apartment balconies with grills). ## 2. Aerodynamic physics

- **Ambient wind as a competing/co-operating flow field.** A conventional ceiling fan forms a bounded recirculation cell: downwash jet → floor impingement → wall-climb return → re-entrainment at the top. In a semi-open space, one or more "walls" of that recirculation loop are missing or replaced by free-stream ambient wind. This converts the fan's flow field from a quasi-closed-room recirculation problem into a **jet-in-crossflow** problem: the fan's downwash jet is a roughly vertical jet issuing into a horizontal (or oblique) ambient crossflow. Jet-in-crossflow theory (well documented for exhaust stacks, VTOL aircraft downwash, and industrial jets, though not specifically validated for ceiling fans outdoors) predicts that the jet bends over in the direction of the ambient wind, its centreline traces a curved trajectory governed by the **jet-to-crossflow momentum/velocity ratio**, and it spreads/dilutes faster than a jet in still air because the crossflow strips vorticity off the jet column. [UNVERIFIED — extrapolated from general jet-in-crossflow literature located during this research (e.g., cross-flow fan and VTOL ducted-fan studies); no ceiling-fan-specific experimental or CFD paper on outdoor wind interaction was found.]
- **No fixed return-air wall.** With one or more sides open, the "wall-climb and re-entrainment" portion of the indoor recirculation loop (the same physical effect researched in Condition 11, open-plan/no-nearby-wall spaces) is weakened or absent, so the air the fan "throws" largely escapes outward rather than recirculating. The practical implication is the same qualitative effect documented for open-plan spaces: breeze spreads thin, and perceived velocity at occupant height drops faster with distance from the fan centre than in an enclosed room.
- **Ground/floor effect still applies** inside the semi-open footprint (furniture, floor, parapet act as a partial boundary), but is asymmetric: the open side(s) offer no rebound, so the floor jet "leaks" outward through the open face rather than building the toroidal recirculation seen in a 4-wall room.
- **Richardson/Archimedes number and buoyancy** are generally less dominant outdoors because the space is thermally connected to the external environment (no stratified hot-air ceiling layer builds up the way it does in a sealed top-floor room — see Condition 13), but radiant heat gain on a sit-out roof slab can still create a local thermal boundary layer that the fan recirculates onto occupants, similar to Condition 15.
- **Quantified effects:** No peer-reviewed or standards-body data quantifying % air-delivery loss, velocity-at-occupant-height change, or noise change specifically for a ceiling fan in ambient wind/crossflow was found in this search pass. [UNVERIFIED] This is a genuine literature gap (see Section 7 and Section 9) — IS 374:2019's standard air-delivery and service-value test (BEE "m³/min/W") is run in a closed test room with no imposed crossflow, so certified air-delivery numbers do not reflect real outdoor performance.

## 3. Role of speed / RPM / frequency

- No condition-specific published guidance was found on an "optimum speed band" for outdoor/crossflow operation. Generic household fan practice (BLDC remote-control step speeds, typically 1–5 or more with Atomberg/Superfan/Crompton BLDC ranges — see Section 6) applies unchanged; users are expected to simply run the fan at a higher step to compensate for wind dilution, which is an unoptimised brute-force response.
- **Resonance/vibration risk may be elevated outdoors**: gusty ambient wind can impose unsteady aerodynamic side-loads on the blades and on a wall-mounted bracket (wall fans are cantilevered off a single wall bracket rather than a central downrod, which is structurally more sensitive to lateral gust loading). This is a plausible mechanical-engineering inference, not a cited finding. [UNVERIFIED]
- **Updraft/reverse mode, pulsed "natural breeze" modes:** manufacturer marketing (Atomberg, and the broader BLDC-fan category) advertises these modes widely for indoor comfort and winter destratification, but no source specific to outdoor/semi-open operation was found.

## 5. Materials and manufacturing

- **Corrosion resistance is the dominant materials requirement**, more than for any other condition in this set, because the fan is continuously exposed to humidity, wind-blown rain spray, dust and (in coastal India) salt air.
  - Aluminium blades are commonly marketed as corrosion-resistant versus pressed/painted steel: e.g. Orient Electric's Ujala Prime and Apex Prime ranges advertise "corrosion-resistant" / "rust-proof" aluminium blades; Bajaj markets "corrosion-resistant aluminium blades, powder-coated finishes"; Havells' FAB BLDC range advertises "ribbed, corrosion-resistant blades" for humid conditions ([Flipkart – Orient Ujala Prime](https://www.flipkart.com/orient-electric-ujala-prime-bee-star-rated-1200-mm-3-blade-ceiling-fan/p/itmf66e3e467ca10), [Orient Apex Prime](https://orientelectric.com/products/apex-prime-bee-star-rated-ceiling-fan), general retailer copy — marketing claims, not independently tested data; treat as [UNVERIFIED claims] pending lab corrosion-test evidence).
  - Atomberg's own product marketing describes a "rust-free build" and "moisture protection and sealed winding" for durability in humid environments, and a wide voltage tolerance (165–285 V) ([Atomberg product pages via Croma/Atomberg.com listings](https://atomberg.com/collections/atomberg-ceiling-fans) — marketing claim, [UNVERIFIED] independent test data).
  - A retailer-listed model, the "Tezz Star Pro," is explicitly marketed as suitable for "kitchen, veranda, balcony, and small rooms" with a BEE 3-star rating ([Allykart product listing](https://www.allykart.com/tezz-star-pro-ceiling-fan)) — again a retail/marketing claim, not an independent verification that it is weatherproof-rated.
- **Western/global wet-rated vs damp-rated category** (Hunter, Big Ass Fans/Haiku) gives the clearest documented engineering benchmark available for this condition, even though it is a US/global product category rather than Indian:
  - **IP rating convention:** IPX2/IPX3 = damp-rated (tolerates humidity, not direct water contact — suited to a *covered* porch/veranda/sit-out with no direct rain impingement); IPX4+ (commonly IP44) = wet-rated (tolerates direct rain/spray, suited to open pergolas, poolside, open balconies) ([Today's Fans — Wet Rated Outdoor Ceiling Fans](https://www.todaysfans.com/collections/outdoor-wet), [Hunter Fan — Water Resistant vs Damp Rated](https://hunterfanlatinoamerica.com/en/outdoor-ceiling-fans-water-resistant-vs-damp-rated/), [Hunter Fan blog — Can Outdoor Ceiling Fans Get Wet or Be Rained On?](https://www.hunterfan.com/blogs/hunter-blog/can-outdoor-ceiling-fans-get-wet-or-be-rained-on)).
  - Wet-rated designs use **sealed motor housings, corrosion-resistant finishes/stainless-steel hardware, and ABS/UV-stabilized composite blades** rather than wood or painted steel, specifically to resist warping, drooping and corrosion from rain, humidity and salt air ([Hunter Fan — WeatherMax collection](https://www.hunterfan.com/collections/wet-rated-weathermax-ceiling-fans), [Del Mar Fans — Hunter Outdoor](https://www.delmarfans.com/hunter-ceiling-fans/outdoor/)).
  - Big Ass Fans' **Haiku Outdoor / Haiku L** line (damp-rated, for covered patios/porches/sunrooms with overhead protection) uses precision-balanced **aircraft-grade aluminium airfoils** and claims up to ~17,300–17,500 CFM (≈490 m³/min) at under 35 dBA on the largest 84" model ([bigassfans.com Haiku Outdoor cutsheet](https://bigassfans.com/docs/haiku/haiku/cutsheet-haiku-outdoor-en.pdf), [AuthenTeak listing](https://authenteak.com/big-ass-fans-haiku-84-oil-rubbed-bronze-outdoor-ceiling-fan-universal-mount/)). These are manufacturer datasheet figures, treated here as verified-by-manufacturer-datasheet rather than independently lab-confirmed.
- **No Indian standard found that defines a wet/damp IP-rating class specifically for ceiling fans.** IS 374:2019 (electric ceiling fans, BIS) covers performance, safety, air-delivery and service-value testing for induction and BLDC ceiling fans up to 250 V/125 W, but the search did not surface any clause addressing outdoor/wet-location ingress protection ([BIS IS 374:2019 PDF](https://bis.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/PM_IS-374-final_compressed.pdf), [TUV summary of the Quality Control Order](https://www.tuv.com/regulations-and-standards/en/india-electric-ceiling-type-fans-quality-control-order-2023.html)). This is a confirmed gap, not an assumption: Indian manufacturers appear to rely on general product marketing language ("moisture-resistant," "rust-free") rather than a certified IP-rated wet-location category the way Hunter/Big Ass Fans do in the US market. [Gap — see Section 9.]
- **Manufacturing/cost implication (inference, [UNVERIFIED] cost figures):** a sealed motor housing, stainless fasteners, and UV-stabilised composite blades (vs standard aluminium/steel + paint) would be expected to add bill-of-materials cost versus a standard indoor fan, but no INR cost delta could be sourced for this report.

## 7. Academic and industry research

No peer-reviewed paper, thesis, or CFD study specifically addressing **ceiling-fan performance under ambient outdoor wind / jet-in-crossflow conditions** was located in this research pass, despite targeted searches (e.g. "wind effect on ceiling fan performance outdoor patio CFD," "jet in crossflow ceiling fan ambient wind"). This appears to be a genuine, confirmed literature gap rather than a missed search — the closest matches returned were:

- General ceiling-fan CFD studies in **enclosed rooms** (no ambient wind): transient 3D CFD models of ceiling fans achieving ~83% agreement with measurement using the SST k-ω turbulence model ([ScienceDirect — Transient three-dimensional CFD modelling of ceiling fans](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360132317302743)); CFD studies optimising sweep/blade-count/RPM in a closed room reporting peak velocities of ~1.53 m/s decaying to >0.6 m/s toward the floor ([ResearchGate — Simulation of airflow around ceiling fan using CFD](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377576570_Simulation_Of_Air_Flow_Around_Ceiling_Fan_In_An_Enclosed_Space_By_Using_Cfd)). These establish validated CFD methodology (transient, SST k-ω, MRF or sliding mesh) that a team could extend to an outdoor/crossflow boundary condition, but none of them impose an ambient wind inlet.
- General **jet-in-crossflow and ducted-fan ground/ceiling-effect** literature from adjacent fields (VTOL aircraft, cross-flow fans for HVAC indoor units), which is directionally relevant physics but not ceiling-fan-specific: ducted-fan aerodynamic performance is known to be sensitive to ground/ceiling proximity within ~1.5 rotor radii, and jet flows are known to attach to nearby solid surfaces via the Coandă effect ([MDPI review — Performance and Efficiency of Cross-Flow Fans](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/16/23/7798), [ResearchGate — Aerodynamics of cross-flow fans](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253868528_Aerodynamics_of_cross-flow_fans_and_their_application_to_aircraft_propulsion_and_flow_control)). [UNVERIFIED applicability to household ceiling fans specifically.]
- No IIT/IISc/CEPT/LBNL/CBE Berkeley/ASHRAE RP study specific to outdoor or semi-open ceiling-fan use was found in this pass. [UNVERIFIED — may exist but was not located; a deeper university-repository or ASHRAE RP-project search is recommended as a follow-up.]

**Recommended CFD approach for this condition** (synthesised from the general ceiling-fan CFD literature above, adapted for the missing wall boundary): a transient, sliding-mesh or MRF rotor model with an SST k-ω turbulence closure, open/pressure-outlet boundary conditions on the open face(s) instead of a no-slip wall, and a prescribed ambient-wind inlet velocity profile (e.g. 0.3–3 m/s, varied by direction) to study jet deflection — this is an engineering recommendation extrapolated from the cited enclosed-room CFD methodology, not itself a validated outdoor-fan CFD study.

## 9. Unsolved gaps and opportunities

- **No validated, India-relevant ingress-protection (IP) standard or rating exists for ceiling fans sold into the Indian semi-open/balcony/veranda segment.** IS 374:2019 does not appear to address wet-location testing (confirmed gap per Section 5/8 search). Indian brands rely on generic "corrosion-resistant"/"rust-free" marketing language rather than a certified IPX4+ wet rating the way Hunter/Big Ass Fans do abroad. **Opportunity:** be the first major Indian brand to publish an IP-rated (e.g. IP44-equivalent) wet-rated ceiling/wall fan line with third-party test certification — a credibility and marketing differentiator as well as a genuine durability gap.
- **No quantified understanding (in the literature found) of how ambient outdoor wind actually degrades a ceiling fan's effective cooling at occupant height.** This is a real physics gap: BEE/IS 374 air-delivery and service-value certification is measured in a still-air closed test room, so the certified number does not reflect real sit-out/veranda performance in a crossflow. **Opportunity:** an aerodynamics-led team could be the first to publish (even internally) a wind-tunnel or field dataset of jet deflection vs crossflow velocity ratio for a household ceiling fan — directly useful for a "wind-adaptive" marketing claim and for tuning blade/speed response.
- **Mechanical/structural risk of gust loading on wall-mount brackets** is plausible but unquantified. **Opportunity:** publish a simple gust-load fatigue test protocol for wall-mounted balcony fans (relevant to Condition 28, wobble/vibration, as well).
- **Concept directions (speculative, engineering judgement — mark all quantified "expected benefit" figures as placeholders needing validation, not measured values):**
  1. *IP44-rated sealed BLDC housing with corrosion-resistant blade coating* — mechanism: sealed bearings/winding + hydrophobic/anti-corrosive blade finish; expected benefit: genuine multi-year reliability claim in humid/coastal zones [benefit magnitude UNVERIFIED]; risk: cost increase, BIS/IS 374 recertification; cost impact: moderate BOM increase (exact INR not sourced); novelty vs patents: likely low-risk if sealing method differs from the housing-cover patents found above.
  2. *Wind-adaptive speed control* — mechanism: anemometer or current-draw-based sensing of ambient gust loading feeding into BLDC speed logic to auto-compensate perceived cooling loss in a crossflow; expected benefit: [UNVERIFIED, no baseline data exists yet — this is exactly the data gap identified above]; risk: sensor cost and calibration complexity, no validated control law exists; cost impact: added sensor + firmware; novelty: plausible white space since no existing product found claims this.
  3. *Asymmetric/directional blade pitch to counter known wind direction* — mechanism: similar in spirit to the wall-mount asymmetric design discussed for Condition 07 (off-centre/near-wall mounting), but tuned for a prevailing outdoor wind direction rather than a wall; expected benefit: [UNVERIFIED]; risk: only helps if wind direction is predictable per installation, which it often isn't; cost impact: low if done via blade geometry only; novelty: unexplored in sources found.

## 10. Measurement and validation plan

- **Instrumentation:** a grid of hot-wire or vane anemometers at occupant height (seated ~1.1 m, lying ~0.3–0.6 m) across the semi-open floor plan, plus one or more reference anemometers placed to measure ambient wind speed/direction independent of the fan (e.g., upwind of the space) so fan-induced velocity can be separated from ambient wind. Smoke/fog visualisation (or a handheld anemometer sweep) to visualise jet deflection direction under different ambient wind headings. A sound meter for dBA at 1 m and at occupant locations. A power meter to confirm input wattage at each speed step matches BEE-declared service value. Humidity/temperature probes to document site conditions (relevant to the corrosion/humidity angle, not aerodynamics alone).
- **Protocol suggestion:** repeat the same indoor grid-anemometer method already standard for IS 374/BEE service-value testing, but run it: (a) in a fully enclosed room (baseline, matches certified rating), (b) in the same room with one wall open (simulating a sit-out/open balcony), and (c) outdoors with a measured ambient crossflow — comparing (a) vs (b) vs (c) isolates the open-wall effect from the added ambient-wind effect.
- **Metrics to report:** velocity field map (m/s) at occupant height across the floor plan under each ambient-wind condition; % drop in average occupant-height velocity vs the enclosed-room baseline; jet-deflection angle vs crossflow velocity ratio; dBA; input power; any wobble/vibration (accelerometer) under gusty conditions.
- **CFD comparison:** build the transient sliding-mesh/MRF + SST k-ω model validated in the enclosed-room literature (Section 7), replace one or more room-boundary walls with a pressure/velocity-inlet boundary representing ambient wind, and validate against the field measurements above before trusting the CFD for concept screening.

## 12. Summary

**Executive summary:** Ceiling fans in Indian balconies, verandas, sit-outs and covered terraces operate in a fundamentally different aerodynamic regime from an enclosed room — ambient wind converts the fan's bounded recirculation loop into an open jet-in-crossflow problem, and the open wall(s) remove the return-air path that normally rebuilds air delivery at occupant height. Despite this being a very common Indian housing feature, this research pass found **no dedicated academic, CFD, or standards-body literature quantifying the effect**, and **no major Indian fan brand publishing a certified wet/damp IP-rated outdoor fan line**, unlike the mature wet-rated/damp-rated category already established by Hunter Fan and Big Ass Fans (Haiku Outdoor) internationally. Indian brands currently address this condition only through generic "corrosion-resistant"/"rust-free" marketing claims rather than certified ingress protection or any documented aerodynamic adaptation to ambient wind.

**Top 5 insights:**
1. The core physics reframes this condition as jet-in-crossflow / open-recirculation-loop, not a new "room shape" — but no ceiling-fan-specific experimental or CFD study of this was located, leaving the governing quantitative relationships (deflection angle vs velocity ratio, % velocity loss at occupant height) unverified.
2. IS 374:2019 testing is done in a still-air enclosed room, so BEE-certified air-delivery/service-value numbers likely overstate real performance in this condition — a credible, India-specific research/marketing opportunity.
3. No major Indian brand currently offers a certified wet-rated (IPX4+/IP44) ceiling or wall fan; the international precedent (Hunter WeatherMax, Big Ass Fans Haiku Outdoor) shows the materials/sealing playbook (sealed motor, stainless hardware, UV-stable composite or aircraft-grade aluminium blades) already exists and could be localised to 230 V/50 Hz Indian fans.
4. Patent landscape search in this pass was shallow and mostly surfaced US design patents on housing shape and separate protective-cover utility patents (lower overlap risk for a built-in sealed design); a full InPASS/Espacenet/WIPO search is still needed before any design-around claim is relied on.
5. No wind-adaptive sensing or control product was found for this condition — an open white-space opportunity, contingent on first generating the baseline velocity/crossflow dataset this report could not find in existing literature.

**Open questions / what could not be found:**
- Quantified air-delivery or occupant-velocity loss in outdoor/crossflow conditions for any ceiling fan (no source found — recommend original field/CFD study, Section 10).
- Whether IS 374:2019 or any other Indian standard defines, or BIS plans to define, a wet-location/IP-rated test class for ceiling fans (not found; may require direct inquiry to BIS).
- Full bibliographic and legal-status detail (priority date, current status, independent claims) for the patents listed in Section 8 — only search-result snippets were reviewed, not the full patent documents.
- Market sizing/prevalence data for balcony/veranda/sit-out fan installations in India (no demographic or market-research source found).
- Any India-specific academic (IIT/IISc/CEPT) study of outdoor or semi-open ceiling-fan airflow (none found in this pass; a university-repository-focused follow-up search is recommended).
