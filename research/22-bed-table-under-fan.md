# Ceiling Fan Aerodynamics Dossier — Condition 22: Bed, Dining Table or Desk Directly Under the Fan (Ground/Surface Effect)

*Prepared for Atomberg "AtomQuest 2026". Condition: a raised surface 0.5–0.8 m high (bed, dining table, desk) sits directly beneath the ceiling fan, shortening the jet throw to the occupant zone.*

## 1. Condition definition

- **Geometry**: Fan mounted at typical Indian ceiling height (2.6–3.2 m), blade plane ~2.3–2.9 m above floor. A bed (mattress top ≈ 50–75 cm, commonly cited as 60–65 cm [UNVERIFIED — single blog source, no standards body] [arcedior.com](https://arcedior.com/blog/bed-sizes-in-india-guide)), a dining table (standard height 73–76 cm with chair seat 45–48 cm) [arcedior.com](https://arcedior.com/blog/dining-table-size-guide-india), [durian.in](https://www.durian.in/inspiration-and-ideas/standard-dining-table-dimensions-guide), [flowyline.com](https://flowyline.com/blogs/for-diy-ers/how-tall-should-a-dining-table-be), or a desk (80 cm desk height is conventional; standing desks 100–120 cm) [flowyline.com](https://flowyline.com/blogs/for-diy-ers/how-tall-should-a-dining-table-be) sits directly in the fan's downwash path, cutting the free-jet throw distance roughly in half versus an open floor.
- **Effective throw**: Instead of the ~2.3–2.6 m fan-to-floor throw assumed in most ceiling-fan studies, the jet travels only ~1.5–2.0 m before hitting the bed/table surface, then must redevelop as a wall jet across that surface at occupant level (torso/face height when lying or seated).
- **Prevalence in India**: This is arguably the single most common ceiling-fan use case in Indian homes — the fan over the bed (bedrooms) and the fan over the dining table (living/dining rooms) are near-universal placements, driven by room layout constraints (fan is centred on the room, and the bed/table is also centred for space efficiency). No national survey quantifying "% of fans with furniture directly below" was found; this is treated as common domain knowledge rather than a cited statistic — flagged **[UNVERIFIED — no field-survey data located]**.

## 2. Aerodynamic physics

- **Free jet vs. impinging jet**: A ceiling fan produces a shrouded, swirling turbulent jet that stays roughly within the blade-tip diameter down to about 0.6 m above the floor, then spreads radially in a thin high-speed outwash layer. Research at UC Berkeley's Center for the Built Environment (CBE) measured this directly: the jet does not widen beyond the blade diameter until below 0.6 m from the floor, and at 0.3 m above the floor the (circumferentially averaged) speed drops to roughly half of its peak value (~0.7 m/s in their test fan/speed) due to impingement losses [escholarship.org — Gao, Zhang, Arens et al., "Ceiling fan air speeds around desks and office partitions"](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3pq2j9mh/qt3pq2j9mh_noSplash_f340ee940d7072e92c55ab3be91ca070.pdf).
- **Effect of inserting a surface (table/bed) mid-jet**: When a table (or by extension a bed) sits in the jet path, the same study found that the jet impinges on the table top and is deflected outward over its edges "quite equally," producing a new, shallower wall-jet layer directly on the furniture surface instead of at the floor — i.e., the impingement plane moves up from floor level to furniture-top level, and the occupant (lying on the bed or seated at the table) sits exactly inside that new, shallow, high-shear impingement layer rather than in the calmer, fully-developed core of the jet [ResearchGate — same paper, figure 3 "flow boundaries with and without a table"](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Visualization-of-the-fan-jets-flow-boundaries-with-and-without-a-table_fig3_319196046).
- **Governing phenomena**:
  - *Shortened jet development length* → the jet has less distance to decay and spread before impingement, so velocity at the impingement plane is higher and more spatially non-uniform than at the floor.
  - *Impingement + wall-jet formation*: classic confined/impinging-jet physics applies — a stagnation region directly under the hub axis, a radial wall-jet that accelerates just past stagnation then decays, and elevated turbulence intensity in the stagnation and rebound zones. Studies of confined impinging jets show turbulence intensity rising with jet-to-surface distance ratio (H/W) and with Reynolds number in the shear layer, and note that rebounding flow in the stagnation region further raises local turbulence [ResearchGate — confined impinging jet reviews](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/223621683_An_experimental_and_numerical_investigation_on_a_confined_impinging_air_jet_at_high_Reynolds_numbers), [Springer — axisymmetric wall jet in confined impingement](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pof/article/29/2/025102/832822/Axisymmetric-wall-jet-development-in-confined-jet).
  - *Ground/ceiling-effect analogy*: UAV ducted-fan ground-effect literature (directly transferable actuator-disk physics, not fan-specific but same disk-in-proximity-to-a-plane problem) shows thrust and flow characteristics change non-monotonically with clearance ratio h/D, with a sensitive regime at h/D ≈ 0.5–1.0 and a thrust-minimum near h ≈ 1R; below h/D ≈ 0.75 ground-proximity effects become significant [MDPI Drones, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/drones10050314), [Science.gov / confinement height review, 0<H/D<1 regime](https://www.science.gov/topicpages/j/jet+impingement+flow). For a 1200 mm fan (D=1.2 m) with a bed/table at h≈1.8–2.0 m below the blades, h/D ≈ 1.5–1.7 — outside the strongest ground-effect zone but still well inside the "short throw" regime documented by CBE (<2.3 m typical full-room throw).
  - *Gustiness / draft sensation*: Because the occupant is inside the shallow, high-shear wall-jet layer rather than the calmer core, perceived air movement is gustier and less uniform — consistent with ASHRAE 55's draft-risk framework, where draft sensation depends jointly on mean speed, turbulence intensity, and local air temperature, and sensitivity is highest on uncovered skin (head/neck, ankles/feet) [SimScale ASHRAE 55 summary](https://www.simscale.com/blog/ashrae-55-thermal-comfort/), [ASHRAE 55 addenda](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/55_2020_h_20221230.pdf).
- **Quantified effects found in literature**:
  - Floor-impingement speed ≈ 50% of peak jet speed at 0.3 m above floor (CBE study) [escholarship.org](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3pq2j9mh/qt3pq2j9mh_noSplash_f340ee940d7072e92c55ab3be91ca070.pdf).
  - Jet remains within blade-tip diameter until <0.6 m from the impingement surface — i.e., with a table/bed present, this near-surface "core" zone sits right at body height instead of near the floor (same source).
  - Ground-effect thrust change up to ~26% versus open space in UAV ducted-fan analogues at small clearances [MDPI Drones](https://doi.org/10.3390/drones10050314) — presented as an aerodynamic-analogy figure, **not** a validated number for household ceiling fans **[UNVERIFIED for this specific application]**.
  - No India-specific or fan-specific (vs. UAV-rotor) quantified % air-delivery-loss or dB(A) number for "fan directly over a bed/table" was located in the time available; this is a **documented gap** (see Section 9).

## 3. Role of speed / RPM / frequency

- Lower RPM reduces both the mean jet velocity and (generally) the turbulence intensity at the impingement plane, directly lowering draft risk — consistent with why virtually every BLDC smart fan markets a dedicated low-speed "Sleep" setting for exactly this placement (bed under fan).
- **Sleep/breeze modes in the market**: Atomberg's BLDC smart fans (e.g., Aris) offer discrete Sleep, Boost and Guest modes with up to six speed steps; sleep mode is typically implemented as a scheduled step-down (reduce speed by one step every hour) rather than a continuous/adaptive control [Croma — Best BLDC fans 2026 roundup](https://www.croma.com/unboxed/best-bldc-fans-in-india-2026-top-energy-saving-picks-for-every-home). Crompton's Energion Roverr and comparable models include an explicit Sleep Mode for quieter night operation [vsctimes.com comparison](https://vsctimes.com/atomberg-vs-crompton-bldc-fan/). Bajaj markets a "Breeze Mode" that varies speed every ~30 seconds to mimic natural wind rather than running at one constant RPM [search synthesis, same query] — this is a marketing-stated mechanism; no independent lab validation of its comfort benefit was found **[UNVERIFIED — marketing claim]**.
- **Global precedent — Big Ass Fans Haiku / SenseMe**: SenseMe uses temperature, humidity, and PIR motion sensing to infer whether someone is in bed and asleep, then automatically steps the fan down, explicitly marketed for the "fan directly over the bed" scenario; the company also cites a patented low-noise motor design (sound-chamber tested) as the basis for its "quiet even at high speed" claim [bigassfans.com blog](https://bigassfans.com/blog/the-best-ceiling-fan-for-a-bedroom-quiet-efficient-and-built-for-sleep/), [smarthomesolver.com review](https://smarthomesolver.com/reviews/haiku-l-series-fan-review/). These are company claims, not independently published test data — flagged as **marketing claims**, not verified lab results.
- **No resonance/beat-frequency literature specific to this condition** was found; general fan-system vibration/resonance (downrod + blade natural frequency vs. RPM) is covered in Condition 28's brief, not duplicated here.
- **Reverse/updraft mode** is not relevant to this condition (it is a destratification tool, Condition 13), and no source links it specifically to furniture-under-fan placement.

## 4. Blade and fan geometry levers

Ranked by expected relevance to a *short-throw, impinging-jet-onto-a-person* scenario (based on the physics in Section 2, since no condition-specific ranking study exists):

| Rank | Lever | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Blade pitch angle / twist distribution | Controls the velocity profile and swirl handed to the jet before it even starts developing; a flatter, more axial exit profile reduces the peak core velocity that would otherwise slam into a close surface. |
| 2 | Sweep diameter (relative to bed/table width) | Determines whether the impingement footprint covers the whole sleeping/seating area evenly or creates a sharp hot/cold-spot pattern at the bed edges. |
| 3 | Number of blades / chord | Affects how "lumpy" (blade-count-periodic) the downwash is; more, narrower blades tend to smooth the jet, reducing localized gusts felt at close range. |
| 4 | Downrod length / blade-to-ceiling clearance | Sets the *actual* throw distance available before the furniture surface — shorter downrod (if ceiling allows) effectively worsens this condition; longer downrod (if ceiling height allows) partially restores jet development length. |
| 5 | Tip shape (winglets, rounded, serrated) | Influences tip-vortex strength and the turbulence intensity carried into the near-surface shear layer — serrated/winglet tips used elsewhere in the industry to cut blade-tip noise and vortex strength could plausibly reduce gustiness, but no condition-specific test data was found **[UNVERIFIED]**. |
| 6 | Hub size / motor-housing shape | Minor second-order effect on the core-flow blockage directly under the axis (relevant mainly for stagnation-zone turbulence right at the centre of the bed). |
| 7 | Airfoil section / camber | Affects efficiency (service value) more than near-field turbulence structure; still relevant to overall Hz-per-m³/min economics. |

No manufacturer documentation or peer-reviewed source was found that explicitly optimizes any of these levers *for* the furniture-under-fan case; this ranking is a physics-based inference, not a cited finding.

## 9. Unsolved gaps and opportunities

- **No product found that senses the *furniture* geometry** (bed vs. table vs. desk, height, position relative to fan) and adapts blade pitch, speed profile, or airflow direction accordingly — existing "Sleep mode" products (Atomberg, Crompton, Big Ass Fans SenseMe) adapt *speed over time or occupancy*, not the *aerodynamic profile* to compensate for a shortened, impinging jet. This is the clearest gap identified.
- **No independently published quantified comfort/draft data** exists (that this search located) for the specific case of a ceiling fan over a bed at Indian ceiling heights/fan sweeps — the closest analogue (CBE desk study) is an office desk/partition, not a bed, and uses a different fan model. Gap is both physics-data and product-feature.
- **No validated low-noise, low-turbulence blade geometry specifically optimized for the <2 m, surface-impingement throw** was found; all "sleep mode" products solve comfort by *slowing down* rather than by *reshaping* the jet to reduce turbulence intensity at a given mean velocity — leaves headroom for an aerodynamically differentiated approach (e.g., twist/pitch profile that produces a wider, lower-peak-velocity, lower-turbulence-intensity jet specifically suited to short throw).

**3–5 concept directions** (aerodynamics-led, consistent with the evidence above — these are proposed concepts, not sourced claims):

1. **Surface-proximity-adaptive blade pitch/twist profile** — a blade (or smart-pitch mechanism) designed to flatten/widen the exit velocity profile when the fan "knows" (via a simple ultrasonic/IR distance sensor at the hub, as flagged relevant in Section 11) that a reflective/close surface sits within ~2 m, trading peak velocity for uniformity to cut draft/gustiness at bed height. *Benefit*: potentially reduces turbulence-intensity-driven draft complaints without reducing total air delivery (unverified estimate — no quantified benefit number available, needs lab testing). *Risk*: added mechanical complexity/cost; patent landscape around adaptive-pitch fans needs the fuller InPASS search flagged in Section 8. *Novelty*: medium-high versus the sleep-mode-by-speed-only prior art found.
2. **Sleep-specific low-turbulence blade set (fixed geometry)**, i.e., a distinct blade attachment or model variant whose twist/camber is tuned (via CFD, Section 10) for the 1.5–2 m throw/impingement regime rather than the ~2.3 m open-floor regime most blades are implicitly designed for. *Benefit*: a software-free, lower-cost differentiator; *Risk*: single-purpose blade reduces flexibility for rooms without furniture under the fan; *Cost*: low incremental tooling cost if done as a trim variant.
3. **Occupancy + proximity sensor fusion for automatic mode selection** (extending existing Sleep-mode logic) — combine the IMU/current-draw self-diagnostics increasingly built into BLDC controllers with a simple presence sensor to auto-select the lowest comfortable speed when a bed is detected occupied, closing the gap versus Big Ass Fans' SenseMe but at Indian BEE/IS-374-compliant cost points. *Benefit*: convenience + measurable energy saving (not quantified here); *Risk*: false positives/negatives in sensing; *Novelty*: low-medium (SenseMe prior art exists, though not BEE-certified/Indian-market).
4. **Winglet/serrated-tip blade variant aimed at reducing tip-vortex-driven turbulence** at short throw — borrowing a noise-reduction lever already used elsewhere in the industry (per Section 4) but validating it specifically for the impingement-zone turbulence-intensity metric rather than just dB(A). *Benefit/Risk/Cost*: unverified without dedicated CFD/lab test; flagged as the most speculative of the five.

(Only four distinct directions are offered here, short of the 3–5 asked for in volume terms but each is substantively differentiated; a fifth would require data not available in this pass, e.g., a validated "oscillating/alternating blade pitch" concept, which is not included because no supporting evidence for feasibility was found.)

## 10. Measurement and validation plan

- **Cheap real-room test**: Replicate the CBE desk-study method at bed/table scale — mount a representative 1200 mm BLDC fan at standard Indian ceiling height, place a bed/table at 0.5–0.8 m height directly beneath, and grid-map air speed at 0.1 m height intervals from the furniture surface up to 1.2 m, using a hot-wire or vane anemometer array (per CBE methodology) [escholarship.org](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3pq2j9mh/qt3pq2j9mh_noSplash_f340ee940d7072e92c55ab3be91ca070.pdf).
- **Instruments**: multi-point hot-wire/hot-sphere anemometers (for turbulence intensity, not just mean speed — critical for the draft-risk metric per ASHRAE 55), smoke/fog visualization to see the impingement/wall-jet pattern over the furniture, a sound-level meter at ear height (bed) for dB(A), and a power meter to log the BLDC controller's actual draw at each speed step.
- **Metrics to report**: mean velocity and turbulence intensity at 3 heights above the furniture surface (ankle/foot, torso, head-equivalent), draft-risk index per ASHRAE 55 (combining speed, turbulence intensity, and local temperature), dB(A) at ear height, and power (W) per speed step — enabling a full service-value-at-the-body comparison rather than the standard room-average service value.
- **CFD validation target**: model the fan rotor (MRF or sliding mesh, as used generically in adjacent ceiling-fan CFD literature — not condition-specific sourcing) plus an explicit solid furniture surface at 0.5–0.8 m, validate predicted velocity/turbulence profiles above the furniture against the hot-wire grid data above, before trusting CFD-only blade redesign iterations.

- **Relevant and already partially implemented in market**: PIR motion/occupancy sensing combined with temperature/humidity (Big Ass Fans SenseMe) to infer "someone is in bed, asleep" and auto-step the fan down [bigassfans.com](https://bigassfans.com/blog/the-best-ceiling-fan-for-a-bedroom-quiet-efficient-and-built-for-sleep/). No Indian-market product found with an equivalent closed-loop implementation — current Indian "Sleep modes" (Atomberg, Crompton) appear to be user-selected, time-scheduled step-downs rather than sensor-closed-loop [Croma roundup](https://www.croma.com/unboxed/best-bldc-fans-in-india-2026-top-energy-saving-picks-for-every-home).
- **Proposed but unverified**: a simple downward-facing ultrasonic or ToF distance sensor at the fan hub could directly measure the presence and height of furniture below (distinguishing "bed at 0.6 m" from "open floor at 2.6 m") and feed that into an adaptive speed/pitch strategy (Concept 1, Section 9). No existing commercial fan with this specific sensing approach was found in this search — treated as a novel, unvalidated proposal.
- IMU-based wobble sensing (relevant to Condition 28) is not directly applicable here except insofar as any adaptive-pitch mechanism would need to avoid introducing new imbalance/wobble modes.

## 12. Summary

**Executive summary**: A bed, dining table or desk placed directly beneath a ceiling fan — one of the most common fan placements in Indian homes — shortens the fan's jet development length from the roughly 2.3–2.6 m assumed in most published studies to roughly 1.5–2.0 m, moving the jet's impingement/wall-jet zone from the floor up to furniture-top height, exactly where the occupant's body sits. The best available evidence (a UC Berkeley CBE desk/partition study) shows that inserting a surface into the jet path shifts the high-shear, higher-turbulence impingement layer onto that surface, which plausibly explains the common complaint of "gusty," uneven, or disruptive airflow when sleeping or working directly under a fan — though no study located in this pass quantifies that effect specifically for a bed, and no Indian or global product was found that adapts its *aerodynamic profile* (versus simply its speed) to this condition. Current market "Sleep mode" and "Breeze mode" features (Atomberg, Crompton, Bajaj, Big Ass Fans' SenseMe) all solve this by time- or occupancy-based speed reduction, not by blade-geometry or jet-shaping changes, leaving a genuine, evidence-supported opportunity for an aerodynamics-led differentiator.

**Top 5 insights**:
1. The jet's impingement plane — and with it the highest-turbulence, most "gusty" zone — moves from the floor to the furniture-top surface when a bed/table/desk sits under the fan (CBE study).
2. Existing comfort features in this use case (Sleep/Breeze modes) are speed-management solutions, not jet-shaping/blade-geometry solutions — a clear differentiation gap.
3. Draft discomfort in this condition is governed as much by turbulence intensity as by mean velocity (ASHRAE 55 draft-risk framework), yet market specs report only mean air delivery (CMM), not turbulence — a metrics gap worth exploiting in both R&D and marketing.
4. Ground/ceiling-effect aerodynamics (from the UAV ducted-fan literature, used here only as an analogy) suggests clearance ratio h/D is a first-order variable; Indian bed/table-under-1200mm-fan geometries sit at h/D ≈ 1.5–1.7, outside the most extreme ground-effect zone but still within CBE's documented "short throw, no further spreading" regime.
5. The patent landscape search in this pass was incomplete (US-centric, no InPASS/Espacenet pass completed) — before committing to any specific adaptive-pitch or proximity-sensing concept, a full patent search is required.

**Open questions / what could not be found**:
- No India-specific or bed-specific (vs. office-desk) quantified air-speed/turbulence data for this exact condition.
- No independently verified patent numbers/claims for Big Ass Fans' "patented silent motor" or for the referenced twisted-blade low-RPM patents.
- No BEE-database cross-check of the Atomberg/Crompton spec figures cited in Section 6 (retailer-listed values only).
- No Indian academic (IIT/IISc/CEPT) study specific to this condition was located in this pass.
- No quantified estimate of market size/frequency ("% of Indian homes with a bed or table directly under the fan") beyond general domain plausibility.
